# Compose a LinkedIn announcement

Read for LinkedIn copy only, alongside distribution.md and the publication’s active feedback. Rules checked 2026-10-15; refresh official constraints when used. This covers organic feed posts, not articles or ads.

## Platform and delivery constraints

- A feed post permits up to 3,000 characters. Count the complete delivered text, including the article URL, separators, hashtags, and line breaks; do not apply X's 23-character URL rule. GoodSender may impose a lower budget. [LinkedIn posting help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a528176)
- `Copy` excludes the article link because GoodSender appends it. Do not promise “link in comments”: that requires a separate supported and authorized action and conflicts with this delivery shape.
- Use plain text with paragraph breaks; do not rely on Markdown headings, bold markers, or link syntax rendering. Avoid decorative Unicode fonts. Do not assume typed mentions resolve, or that carousels, documents, polls, or media can be sent through this connector. Verify capability before preparing a deliverable that depends on it.
- Feed previews vary by device and format. Put the useful premise first; do not claim a fixed “see more” character cutoff. Check a rendered preview when available, without pretending a blog preview is a LinkedIn preview.

## Editorial defaults, not platform requirements

Start with a concrete professional problem, decision, or supported insight. The first one or two lines should make the subject and relevance clear without a manufactured cliffhanger. Then give a useful takeaway or example from the article, followed by a reason to read the full piece. Make it worthwhile even for someone who does not click.

A starting shape is three to five short paragraphs: premise, explanation or example, practical implication, and one CTA. Use fewer if the idea is simple. A short list can make steps or comparisons easier to scan; do not make every sentence a separate paragraph or turn a small announcement into the full article. There is no prescribed optimal length: the goal, evidence, and saved author preferences decide.

Write for the connected identity: a person can speak as “I” only when the experience is theirs; a company Page should not acquire a fictional personal story. Avoid engagement bait (“comment YES”), invented vulnerability, generic motivation, and inflated claims. Ask a specific question only if the author actually wants a discussion; otherwise close with what the article helps the reader do.

Default to zero to three specific hashtags only when they help label the topic or match the author's practice. This is an editorial default, not a ranking rule. Use emoji sparingly if they belong to the voice. Keep the article link in the supported delivery location rather than following an unverified algorithm hack.

## Community advice and its limits

Buffer’s [analysis of its own LinkedIn Page posts](https://buffer.com/resources/linkedin-posts/) reported associations between format, hashtags, and engagement. It is one Page's observational experience, not a causal rule for every personal profile. Use it as a reason to test relevant topic labels and useful native context, not to mandate a hashtag count.

Its broader [2026 report](https://buffer.com/resources/state-of-social-media-engagement-2026/) also warns that patterns reflect Buffer users and cannot fully separate causation. Compare results against this author's goal: article visits or relevant conversations may matter more than reactions. Do not claim that moving links to comments, a particular hook formula, or a fixed length guarantees reach.

## Example shape

Illustrative source brief: an article contains a release checklist covering ownership, rollback triggers, and decision records. These are example premises, not claims about an actual post.

`Copy`:

```text
Before a release starts, who has the authority to stop it?

The checklist in this article makes three decisions explicit:
• Who owns the release
• What triggers a rollback
• Where the decision is recorded

Use it to frame your next release review. The full article walks through each decision.
```

The service appends the article link. Adapt the example to verified source material and the publication's voice; do not invent a team outcome or hands-on experience to make it more personal.

Before saving: useful opening, concrete takeaway, correct author identity, evidence for every claim, one clear next step, readable paragraphs, no duplicate URL, and effective text within the live budget. Re-read saved copy and selection as described in distribution.md.
