# Compose an X announcement

Read for X copy only, alongside distribution.md and the publication’s active feedback. Rules checked 2026-10-15; refresh official constraints when used. This covers organic blog announcements, not ads.

## Platform and delivery constraints

- Standard posts have a 280-character limit. Longer posts require Premium; do not assume either the account or GoodSender supports them. Use standard length unless both are verified. [X posting help](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/how-to-post)
- X counts a URL as 23 characters, regardless of its displayed length. Reserve space for the appended article link and separator. `Copy` excludes that link; check the complete effective `Text` and GoodSender `Budget`, including whitespace. [X link help](https://help.x.com/en/using-twitter/how-to-tweet-a-link.html)
- Do not certify fit from a raw string length, especially with emoji or non-Latin text. Use the service budget or an X-compatible counter; when offline, leave headroom and label the count provisional. Never solve overflow by silently truncating a qualification or splitting into a thread.
- Plain text and deliberate line breaks are the default. Do not assume Markdown, native mentions, attached media, threads, or Premium formatting are supported by the connector. Verify requested capabilities before promising them. A blog cover does not prove a social attachment exists.

## Editorial defaults, not algorithm rules

Choose one useful idea from the article. Lead with a concrete observation, problem, or finding; give enough substance that the post is useful before the click. Follow with the specific reason to read. Usually one or two sentences, optionally separated by a blank line, is enough. Do not use the full allowance merely because it is available.

Use the author's natural voice and audience vocabulary. Replace “New post!” and “game-changing” with what the reader will learn. Keep necessary caveats and fictional framing. A short post still needs evidence for every new claim.

Default to no hashtags; add a relevant community/event tag when it helps the intended reader and fits their preferences. Do not add trending tags or tag unrelated accounts for reach. Emoji are optional voice choices, not a mandatory hook. Use one primary CTA suited to the goal; do not stack requests to read, follow, repost, and reply. An authentic question can replace a reading CTA when discussion is the goal.

## What community evidence can tell us

Buffer’s [2026 engagement report](https://buffer.com/resources/state-of-social-media-engagement-2026/) observes stronger median engagement for text on X, but studies Buffer-posted content and platform-defined engagement, not guaranteed article clicks. Use a useful text-first announcement as a starting point; do not remove the article link or promise reach from that observation. Test against the publication's own goal and results. Its findings do not establish a universal best length, posting time, or hashtag count.

## Example shape

Illustrative source brief: an article explains how to assign an owner, define a rollback trigger, and record a release decision. These are example premises, not facts about the user's article.

`Copy`:

```text
Who can call a rollback when a release goes wrong?

A practical checklist for naming the owner, setting the trigger, and recording the decision.
```

GoodSender appends the real article link. Do not paste the example or a placeholder URL into saved copy. Adapt the opening to the article's actual content and check the final budget after every edit.

Before saving: one clear idea, supported payoff, readable on its own, appropriate voice, no duplicated article URL, supported delivery format, and complete effective text within budget. Re-read saved copy and selection as described in distribution.md.
